INDONESIA LAB 001

Why 'Indonesia' Contains the Word 'India'

A country that never bordered India or belonged to it.
So why is it called Indonesia?

AYUTA COFFEE LAB · 2026.11
At a glance
Name coined
1850
Meaning
India + islands

The story
01

Europe's 'Indies'

Europeans once called the wide seas and islands between India and China 'the Indies'. So for a long time today's Indonesia was known as the Dutch East Indies, a name given by others.

02

1850: a word made by scholars

In 1850 the British scholar James Logan, working in Singapore, joined the Greek 'Indos' (India) and 'nesos' (island) to write 'Indonesia', meaning 'the islands of India'. At first it was simply a scholarly term for a region.

03

From a scholar's word to a people's name

It was young people dreaming of independence who claimed the word. In the 1928 Youth Pledge (Sumpah Pemuda) they declared 'one motherland, one nation, one language: Indonesia', and with independence in 1945 it became the country's official name. A name they chose for themselves, instead of the colonial one.

04

One more name: Nusantara

Indonesians have an older name, too: the Old Javanese 'Nusantara', roughly 'the outer islands'. It is still used for the island nation, and the new capital being built is also named Nusantara.

One question
Which other countries hide another place in their name?
Show Ayuta's answer

One is right next door. The 'Guinea' in Papua New Guinea comes from Guinea in West Africa: a 16th-century European explorer thought the island's people resembled those of the Guinea coast and called it 'New Guinea'. New Zealand is named after Zeeland, a province of the Netherlands. A country's name carries the gaze of those who looked at it.

Why Vanilla Costs Its Weight in Gold

The flower lasts a single day, and every one is pollinated by hand.
How vanilla became the world's second most expensive spice.

The story
01

Second only to saffron

Vanilla is the fruit of a climbing orchid. Its home is Mexico, but today it is grown across the tropics. Among spices, only saffron costs more. Why so expensive? The answer is in the flower.

02

A flower that lasts one day

A vanilla flower stays open for about a day. If it is not pollinated that day, no fruit forms. In Mexico, insects do the job, but vanilla planted in other countries flowered without bearing fruit.

03

1841: a twelve-year-old's hands

The answer came from Edmond Albius, a twelve-year-old boy on Réunion in the Indian Ocean. With a thin stick and his thumb, he moved pollen flower by flower. Remarkably, vanilla farms still use almost exactly this method today, one flower at a time, by hand.

04

Fresh pods have no aroma

Pollination is only the start. A freshly picked green pod has almost none of the vanilla scent we know. Blanching, sweating, sun and shade drying and conditioning take months before it becomes a dark, supple vanilla bean.

05

Madagascar first, Indonesia second

Madagascar is the world's largest vanilla producer, and Indonesia is second. Wherever it is grown, the process is the same: a one-day flower, hand pollination, months of curing. In the end, vanilla is expensive because of human time.

One question
Is the 'vanilla flavor' in your kitchen real vanilla?
Show Ayuta's answer

Usually not. Most 'vanilla flavor' is vanillin made separately, not taken from vanilla beans. Check the label for 'vanilla bean' or 'vanilla extract'. Real vanilla carries hundreds of aroma compounds besides vanillin, which is why it tastes so much deeper.
